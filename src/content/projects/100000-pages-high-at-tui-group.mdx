## At a glance

**The problem:** The existing website relied heavily on metasearch, a channel associated with some of our lowest margins. We needed a way to reach people while they were discovering destinations and planning a trip.

**My role:** I designed the destination template, generation rules and interface, helped create the Tagus design system, and reviewed the front-end implementation of the booking journey. I worked with the head of e-commerce and colleagues across SEO, product, data and engineering.

**The outcome:** The team demonstrated generation at scale and launched in Portugal and Spain. Our analytics team reported more than 4× organic traffic growth in those markets and reduced dependence on metasearch. Expansion to five markets remained the wider plan.

## Reach travelers before they compare prices

A new TUI e-commerce website was already in development, but its acquisition strategy still depended heavily on metasearch. That concentrated how people reached us and left limited room to compete on the travel experience itself.

Competitors attracted organic visitors through destination pages: places to explore, accommodation to consider and things to do.

Our hypothesis was that useful destination content could bring people into TUI earlier in their planning, then connect that interest to a bookable stay. PageBuilder would let us explore that approach across many places without designing every page separately.

## Design the page and the system behind it

I worked on two connected parts: the destination experience a traveler would see, and the rules used to populate that experience for different places.

PageBuilder combined geographic data, place information, imagery and TUI offers through a shared template. The aim was a repeatable structure with content specific to each destination.

The first version needed to answer four questions:

- What does this place look like?
- Where is it?
- Where could I stay?
- What could I visit?

That gave us a practical scope for the page. It also connected content generation to a customer journey: discovering a place, considering accommodation and moving toward a reservation.

## Explore the larger experience then define the first release

We benchmarked travel platforms and adapted established e-commerce research databases to the travel and booking context. We tested wireframes with users, reviewed interaction heatmaps and critiqued designs together as a team.

The wider vision included weather, bookable activities, branded hotel experiences and richer destination content. These prototypes helped us explore how these ideas could fit into one travel experience.

## Keep discovery and booking connected with Tagus

We developed Tagus design system alongside the page designs. It provided shared components and guidance for the destination pages and the booking journey.

I helped define and document the system for designers and developers, including how components moved from draft designs toward reusable implementations.

This shared foundation also supported collaboration with an external team responsible for checkout. I reviewed the front-end implementation to keep the booking experience aligned with the design.

Tagus gave us a way to carry the same interaction patterns through discovery, accommodation selection and reservation, while preparing the product for more markets.

## Generate at scale, launch in two markets

The proof of concept generated 100,000 pages. That demonstrated the team’s ability to produce pages from the shared template and data, rather than assemble them individually.

The initial launch covered 175 destinations in Portugal and Spain, with a complete path into booking and payment.

The wider plan covered five markets: Portugal, Spain, Brazil, Italy and Greece.

PageBuilder also had a back office for configuring markets and pages. This made the work an operational product as well as a customer-facing template.

The rollout included experiments with content order, session recordings and a feedback chatbot to keep learning from people using the pages.

## What the launch showed

Our analytics team reported organic traffic growing more than 4× in Portugal and Spain, alongside reduced dependence on metasearch.

The project also left reusable capabilities: a geographic data lake, content-generation tooling and cleaner schemas for the legacy back ends. Those were team outcomes that supported the page-generation work.

We presented the approach to colleagues in the Nordics, the UK and Germany to discuss wider adoption across TUI Group.

The broader vision went beyond accommodation, exploring flights and activities within a shared navigation and design language. That remained an ambition for expansion; a change in leadership brought different priorities before we could continue.

## Looking back

I would keep the combination of a reusable page framework and a concrete customer journey. Designing the template, generation rules and booking experience together gave the experiment a path from discovery to purchase that could be reused in other products.

I would involve engineers earlier as we did with the few that sat on opposite desks, while the concepts were still taking shape. Working side by side helped us understand the data and delivery constraints and make progress on the first release.

I would also agree the content and data requirements earlier, so each generated page had a clearer standard for usefulness.

For a similar project, I would define acquisition and booking measures together from the start: not only whether more people arrived, but whether the pages helped them find a suitable stay and complete a reservation.
